medium F-00cf851d-01 · The league's own two Game Books spell the same player two ways

ObservedThe official Week 9 Game Book (New York Giants at Philadelphia Eagles) prints the Giants' starting fullback as 'FB 45 P.Richard'. The official Week 10 Game Book (Washington Commanders at New York Giants) prints 'FB 45 P.Ricard' in its Substitutions list. Same club, same jersey number, same position label, one week apart.
Why it mattersAny availability or replacement tracker that keys a player on a surname string will record two different players and will mis-measure both the Week 9 starting eleven and the Week 10 replacement chain.
What this session didRecorded both spellings verbatim and did not merge them into one identity. The Week 9 to Week 10 set difference on the replacement page therefore shows this player as a departed starter and as a Week 10 substitute, which is how the two documents print him.

critical F-00cf851d-02 · No league depth chart exists, re-checked independently on 2026-11-14

ObservedThe NFL club depth-chart route (checked on the Kansas City Chiefs path) returns the league's own 404 page, titled '404 - Flag on the Play.', not a depth chart.
Why it mattersThe requirement to track whether a 2nd, 3rd or 4th string backup is replacing a starter cannot be satisfied from league data, because the league publishes no first/second/third/fourth-string tier. critical F-00cf851d-03 · The official injury report is a forward-looking designation feed, not a results feed

ObservedThe Week 10 injury report publishes Player, Position, Injuries, Practice Status and Game Status for the current week only. It carries no per-row timestamp, no archive of earlier weeks, and no post-game participation or outcome column.
Why it mattersBacktests and ROI cannot be computed from it. It is an input to a pre-game availability model, not a record of what happened. Using it as a results feed would silently produce a look-ahead artifact: the field values are published before kickoff and describe intent, not outcome.
What this session didPublished every field the league prints, kept blank cells blank, and recorded the limitation on the page rather than treating a blank as a clearance.
Sourcehttps://www.nfl.com/injuries/

critical F-00cf851d-09 · The committed results file is missing the result of every game it has priced since Week 9

Observeddata/raw/games.csv holds 272 scheduled games for 2026. 137 of them have a kickoff date on or before 2026-11-14, and 121 of those carry a result. The 16 without a result are the entire Week 9 slate plus the 2026-11-12 Thursday-night game. The club reconciliation confirms it: only Pittsburgh and Tennessee, the two clubs with no game in the missing set, can be reconciled against the official standings. The other 30 cannot.
Why it mattersA price is recorded for a game whose outcome is not. Any backtest built on this file today silently drops the sixteen most recent games, and any leaderboard that reads it will report performance on a season that is two rounds behind the league. The gap is invisible unless you count played-but-unresulted rows, which no check in the repository does.
What this session didCounted the gap, published the sixteen game ids, and filled thirteen of the outcomes from the league's own schedule pages. The remaining three were left blank rather than sourced from anywhere else.

critical F-00cf851d-11 · There is no opening price anywhere in the repository

Observeddata/raw/games.csv carries exactly one price per market: a closing spread, a closing total, and where present a closing moneyline. There is no opening-price column, no timestamp column and no second snapshot, so there is no way to tell when in the week any of those numbers was taken.
Why it mattersThe requirement is to record both the opening and the closing number for every market originated. That cannot be built from anything committed here, and a store that records only a closing number cannot measure whether a strategy beat a number that was actually available.
What this session didLeft the opening-price column present and empty rather than duplicating the closing value into it, and recorded the gap as a decision the owner has to make about a data source.
