Read this before anything else

NFL.com Terms of Service section 1.3, updated 16 May 2024, prohibits systematic retrieval of content from the Services without express prior written consent. The exact words are: "Systematic retrieval of data or other content from the Services, whether to create or compile, directly or indirectly, a collection, compilation, database, or directory, is prohibited absent our express prior written consent."

That is not a work blocker, it is a permission blocker, and it changes the register. 5 requirements are marked blocked by permission rather than not started, and four suggestions that proposed scheduled capture are marked blocked on permission. It also applies to work already committed: this desk made seven fetches and committed one verbatim capture, so whether that capture stays is an owner decision, not this desk's to make. It is recorded as DD-13 and as owner decision O05. An earlier session reached the same conclusion independently and recorded it as DEC-01; this desk re-read the terms page rather than relying on that record.

Executive summary

No strategy has earned a stake, and nothing on this desk claims an edge. That is the headline, and it is not a failure of effort: it is what the evidence supports today. The repository has built the machinery a competition needs — a price-snapshot store, a verified results feed whose rows recompute and match, a fixed origination order, a joint-evidence gate that refuses to assume independence, a demotion gate that has been replayed, and leaderboard divisions ranked by proper score with uncertainty. What it does not yet have is a single graded event.

The gap is data, and part of it is permission. Of 73 requirements, 16 are met, 33 are partial, 17 have not started and 5 are blocked by NFL.com Terms section 1.3, which prohibits systematic retrieval without express written consent. Read the gate column and the blockages recur. No source obtained records an opening price, so closing-line value cannot be measured and no reward can be earned. No official snap-count source was found, so depth cannot be modelled from playing time. No inactive-list archive exists, so Questionable cannot be calibrated and designation reliability cannot be scored. And the capture that would fix several of those is itself constrained by the terms. These are prerequisites, not refinements.

This session added verified evidence where the official pages allow it. The Week 10 2026 injury report was captured verbatim, hashed and timestamped: 347 rows across 28 clubs. The official reserve list for November 2026 was captured, which closes the question of whether a player missing from the injury report is available — 25 players are not. Week 10's twelve completed games were read from the official schedule, and Week 11's thirteen fixtures with their kickoff times, networks and international designations. The game centre was checked and does publish a venue field, which is the first step towards transcribing every venue.

Two things a reviewer should see before anything else. First, the published Week 10 injury snapshot is stale against the official page by 68 rows: this is published as a drift rather than silently corrected, because the cause is not established. Second, two independent implementations of the same eight origination requirements exist on different inputs, so they can publish different model lines for the same game; that needs an owner decision, not more code.

Nothing on the suggestions board has been implemented. Sixteen suggestions are costed, four candidate paths are laid out and deliberately left unranked, and the decision log records that no path was chosen. Ranking them would substitute the builder's preference for the owner's decision, which is the thing the section exists to prevent.
